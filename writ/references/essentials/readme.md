# README

A README is read by someone meeting a product for the first time. It lets them:

- decide from its first lines whether the product is for them;
- tell why to choose it over the other ways they could do the same work;
- picture using it on their own work, from starting to the result;
- get from deciding to their first result with the README alone;
- tell where to go for more.

What only the product's builders need, such as its design or how to contribute, is not for this
reader. Each item of `doc.md` also applies to a README.

## Benefit

### B-1: What they gain

From the first lines, can the reader tell what they gain from the product?

Why: the reader decides from the first lines whether to read on; when those lines say what the
product does or how it works instead, the reader is left to work out what they gain, and often
leaves first.

### B-2: Who it is for

From the first lines, can the reader tell whether they are someone the product is for?

Why: a reader who cannot place themselves reads on to find out, or leaves a product that would have
served them.

### B-3: Why this one

Can the reader tell what the product gives them that doing the work by hand, or with another tool,
does not?

Why: what only this product gives is the reason to choose it; what every way gives, such as not
losing work, does not decide the choice, and placed first it hides what does.

### B-4: Using it on their own work

Can the reader picture using the product on their own work, from starting to the result, from an
example that shows what they do and what they get back at each point?

Why: a reader who can picture it decides on what they will actually get; an example far from their
own work, or one without what the product gives back, is read as someone else's story.

### B-5: Started from the README alone

Can a reader who has decided to use the product get to their first result with the README alone?

Look for what that needs: what must be in place beforehand, how to install the product, and the
first use.

Why: each place the reader has to look elsewhere is a place they may stop.

### B-6: Where to go for more

Can the reader tell where to find what the README leaves out, such as fuller use, what changed in
each version, and where to ask for help?

Why: a README that holds only what a newcomer needs stays short only when the rest has somewhere the
reader can reach.

## Usability

### U-1: Nothing to learn that neither deciding nor starting needs

Can the reader decide and start without learning names or workings that neither needs?

Why: each name of an inner part or an exception the reader must take in before going on is effort
spent on what they do not use.

### U-2: Getting ready their own way

For each thing that must be in place beforehand, can the reader tell what it is and why it is
needed?

Why: a reader who knows what is needed and why gets it ready in their own way, or sees that they
already have it; one way of getting it ready, given alone, reads as the only way and gives steps to
a reader who does not need them.

## Format

None: a README's sections follow what the product needs to show; what every README must give is
asked above.
