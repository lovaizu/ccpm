# README

The reader is someone meeting the product for the first time. When they finish, they know the benefits the product gives them, decide whether to use it, and can start using it.

- Does the opening give the benefits, the states the reader gets that they would choose the product for over what they would use instead?

    A reader decides whether to use the product by what they get, such as being able to leave the work to it without watching. What the product does toward that, such as "it calls you back only for the decisions that are yours", is a feature even when the user would notice it; mixed into the opening, it leaves the reader to work out what they get.

- Can the reader see at a glance the flow from starting to use it to getting a result, and what they do and get at each stage?

    Each stage leads to a benefit in the opening, and each benefit is reached at some stage. A stage that leads to no benefit is not needed; a benefit that no stage reaches is missing the way to get it.

- Can the reader picture the example as their own work?

    An example far from the reader's work, or one borrowed from another company's product or a well-known app, is read as someone else's case, and the reader cannot see what they would get. A situation close to their work that shows the product's strengths, such as moving a JavaScript app to TypeScript, lets them.

- Can the reader read to the end without knowing the product's internals?

    Names of internal roles and exceptions that do not matter to a first-time reader make them learn what they do not need in order to decide. A reader who asks why the product takes this shape can follow a link to the design doc from the sentence where they ask.

- Can a newcomer get the product and start using it from this README alone, and learn whether they may use it?

    Prerequisites stated with what is needed and why let each reader prepare in their own environment. A single way of preparing reads as if the others could not be chosen, and gives those already prepared a procedure they do not need.
