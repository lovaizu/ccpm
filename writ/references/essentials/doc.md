# Every document

Can the reader grasp the whole by reading from top to bottom, and, when they finish, do what they need to do?

- Is every part something this reader needs in order to decide and do what they came for?

    With who reads, what they decide and do when they finish, and whether they read straight through or skim settled, every part can be judged by whether this reader needs it, and the reader decides and acts without wading through the rest. Take a part away: if the reader would still decide and do the same, it does not belong; if the reader must decide something no part supports, a part is missing. Parts that pass the reader by are often written for someone else, such as a reviewer, an approver, someone rereading or a second reader who needs other things, or they repeat what is already said, tell how the decisions came about, or restate what a tool or another mechanism already enforces and drift from it in time. Looking only for gaps lets a document grow with every fix.

- Can the reader take it in the way they read it, straight through or skimming, without going back or ahead?

    Read straight through, the reader knows from the first sentence what they will gain, follows the argument by the headings, and finds everything a sentence relies on already given. Skimming, they find their part by headings in the words they would look for, and get a list's meaning from items that each make sense alone. Opening sentences about the document itself, a word used before it is explained, a pattern before the example that makes it clear, or a decision's reason, cost or rejected options kept away from it send them back or ahead and cost them their place.

- Can the reader grasp at a glance who takes part, what passes between them, and in what order?

    Seen at a glance, who takes part, what passes on each handoff, who writes and reads each document, and the order, branches and changes of state are the section's key point grasped at once, where prose makes the reader assemble them in their head. A picture of something other than that key point, or holding more than one glance takes in, gives nothing.

- Can the reader tell what is settled, what is assumed, and what is missing?

    A reader who can tell checked facts from assumptions, proposals and decisions, how far each check reached, and where the content itself has a gap or a contradiction, trusts each statement as far as it deserves and can raise what is missing. An unchecked claim stated flatly, a comparison that gathers only the facts favoring one side, or a flaw covered with smooth sentences leads them to act on it unaware.

- Can the reader understand every word without explanation, and read the same word as the same thing?

    Plain words, each used with one meaning, let the reader read on without stopping. Jargon, coined terms, a common word stretched into a figure of speech, one thing called by two names, a person's name where their role is meant, and a name the user never sees each stop them or send them the wrong way.

- Does the text read as a person wrote it for this reader, with the eye drawn only where missing something would hurt?

    A tone suited to the reader and kept to the end lets them trust the text and read it closely, and with few marks their eye stops where it matters. Habits that give away AI writing, such as filler, restating, retreating into generalities or hedged phrasing, make them distrust it and skim; many marked words hide the one that matters, a parenthesis breaks the sentence being followed, and a table used for anything but comparing things on the same points makes them read across cells for what a sentence would say.
