# Every document

The reader is whoever the document is written for. When they finish, they have grasped the whole by reading from top to bottom and can do what they need to do. Every document is judged by these questions, and a README, a design doc or a prompt also by its own file.

- Is every part something this reader needs in order to decide and do what they came for?

    When every part is one this reader needs, they decide and act without wading through the rest; settling who reads, what they decide and do when they finish, and whether they read straight through or skim is what lets each part be judged. Take a part away: if the reader would still decide and do the same, it does not belong; if the reader must decide something no part supports, a part is missing. Parts that pass the reader by are often written for someone else, such as a reviewer or a second reader, or tell how the decisions came about.

- Can the reader take it in the way they read it, straight through or skimming, without going back or ahead?

    Read straight through, the reader knows from the first sentence what they will gain, follows the argument by the headings, and finds everything a sentence relies on already given. Skimming, they find their part by headings in the words they would look for, and get a list's meaning from its items alone. A word used before it is explained, or a decision's reason kept away from it, sends them back or ahead.

- Where the key point is who takes part and what passes between them, can the reader grasp it at a glance?

    Seen at a glance, who takes part, what passes on each handoff, who writes and reads each document, and the order and branches are grasped at once, where prose makes the reader assemble them in their head. A picture of something prose already gives at once, or holding more than one glance takes in, costs a look and gives nothing.

- Can the reader tell what is settled, what is assumed, and what is missing?

    A reader who can tell checked facts from assumptions, proposals and decisions, how far each check reached, and where the content itself has a gap or a contradiction, trusts each statement as far as it deserves and can raise what is missing. An unchecked claim stated flatly, or a flaw covered with smooth sentences, is acted on unaware.

- Can the reader understand every word without explanation, and read the same word as the same thing?

    Plain words, each used with one meaning, let the reader read on without stopping. Jargon, coined terms, or one thing called by two names stop them or send them the wrong way.

- Does the text read as a person wrote it for this reader, with the eye drawn only where missing something would hurt?

    A tone suited to the reader and kept to the end lets them trust the text and read it closely, and with few marks their eye stops where it matters. Habits that give away AI writing, such as filler or restating, make them distrust it and skim, and many marked words hide the one that matters.
