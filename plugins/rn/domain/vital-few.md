# rn's measure of the vital few

The vital few are what a run's people can't reach their goal without. The rest is the trivial many:
it spends the people's time and the user's attention, and brings no one closer. rn keeps the vital
few and cuts the trivial many thoroughly, from what a run aims at and from each deliverable alike,
as a part that stays draws more in. Every judgment in rn rests on this measure, ahead of a best
practice, a template, an issue, a finding, or a habit.

## The goal

- The goal is the steering's `outcome`, whom the run serves and what they do once it's reached,
  and the need that the user picked, its `target`.
- The key results are those of the product vision of the deliverable's product, where the run has
  one, else what the goal says its people do. A key result serves the goal, so where the two pull
  apart, the goal decides.
- What the user decided, their rules among them, stays, as only they change it.

## The questions

Ask each element, such as a deliverable, a decision, a remedy, a step, a point to ask, or a line of a
message, these in order:

1. Where does it come from? What the user decided stays. Questions 2 and 3 decide each other
   element, as the trivial many come in through the other sources, such as an issue's means taken
   as settled.
2. Without it, does a key result get worse in common real use? Answer from a record that shows it,
   such as the user's words, the issue, the product vision, a best practice, the repository's
   precedents, a Round's result, or a script's output. Where none does, cut the element. A second
   output format that none of the people read moves no key result.
3. Of what stays, is it the least? What already meets the need, such as a feature of Claude Code, a
   tool the people have, or a part of the repository, is less than anything made, so take it and
   make only what it lacks. Cut each part beyond the least that holds the key result: a guide that
   misses a step needs the step, not a new section.
4. What in the run does it rest on? Ask each such element again from question 1, a done
   deliverable's decisions and the issue's among them, as the trivial many hide in what's already
   there.

## What doesn't count

- An element's size, or its cost to build, is no ground to keep it. Nor is a finding being correct,
  or an issue, a template, or a best practice saying so: each shows only that the element could be
  made, not that the people need it.
- A guess at what the people might want is no record, as it lets in what no one needs.
- Where the records leave you unsure, choose less. A cut that proves wrong comes back with its fact.

## A judgment

A judgment names, for each element, kept or cut, the question that decides it, and the record that
its answer rests on, as whoever acts on it checks it by them.
