## Practices

### Q1

Listing whom to build for, does the developer find every role that comes to the product with an end of its own, so that they leave out or split in two no one the product must serve?

**Thinking**

Each role is one end that someone brings to the product. Cut the roles by that end, not by who or what takes them: a person and an AI that want the same are one role. A cut by kind hides an end or splits one.

**Steps**

1. Walk through who uses the product, who runs it, who receives what it makes, whom it affects, and each AI or system that calls it, over the product's whole life.
2. Name the only role the user, as the reader learns nothing more from a name made up for it.
3. Ask the user who else comes to the product, and confirm with them each role the request doesn't show.

### Q2

Reading an objective, does the developer see the end its role is after, so that they build toward that end rather than toward a means or a measure of it?

**Thinking**

An objective is the end the role is after in its own work, with the role as its subject. It says nothing of the product, of how the role reaches the end, or of how it would show. A product built to a means or a measure can deliver it while the role misses its end.

**Steps**

1. Ask why of each part of the objective until no further end shows, and write that end. Move a part that says how to Concept, and one that says how it would show to the key results.
2. Confirm with the user an objective that rests on what someone only said or guessed, not on what anyone saw the role do.

### Q3

Weighing a piece of work, does the developer see which one objective it brings closer, or that it brings none closer, so that they know what the work is for or drop it?

**Thinking**

Each piece of work brings exactly one objective closer. A gain under two objectives leaves the developer unable to tell which one the work serves.

**Steps**

1. Merge two objectives that one piece of work brings closer together, and split one whose gains pieces of work bring closer apart.
2. Check by naming, for each change planned or recently made, the one objective it brings closer.

### Q4

After a change, does the developer see from the key results whether to keep it, so that they keep only what brings the role's end closer?

**Thinking**

Key results count what the role does or feels that shows it meets its end: the first how well, and the last how fast. A change that betters a later one counts only while none before it gets worse. A result had fast but not well is no gain, and a count of the product's own output can rise while the role misses its end.

**Steps**

1. List how success or failure would show in what the role does or feels, such as giving up, undoing a result, or working around the product.
2. Make each a share, an average per user, a count, or a time, and name what it's a share or an average of.

### Q5

Weighing the product against what its roles do today, does the developer see why it meets each objective where that way doesn't, so that they build what sets it apart and not a copy of that way?

**Thinking**

Concept holds the few ideas that set the product apart from what its roles do today and make it meet each objective. No one can read a concept off facts: its maker finds it by reacting to tries against the problem. So draw the ideas out of the user one point at a time, by proposals built on their own words. A bare question or a list to pick from gives them nothing to push against, and ideas offered before their own crowd them out.

**Steps**

1. Ask what the roles do today and where it falls short of each objective, and say the user's words back as a reading they can correct.
2. Ask for the user's ideas, and grow each by a proposal, such as a sharper form or a variant, and by why it beats the present way.
3. Argue against the ideas kept, and propose the riskiest assumption under them with how to check it.
4. Stop once at most five ideas stand out, each meeting an objective where the present way doesn't.
5. Keep, where the design lives, each idea tried, whose it was, and why it stayed, went, or merged with another, in the user's words.

### Q6

Changing how the product works inside while its roles see the same, does the developer find the product vision still true, so that it holds as long as the roles' ends do?

**Thinking**

The product vision says only what the roles are after, do, see and receive, and nothing of how the product works inside. Any design that gives the roles the same then leaves it true.

**Steps**

1. Pick one change the design could make while the roles see the same, and rewrite each line that would have to change.
