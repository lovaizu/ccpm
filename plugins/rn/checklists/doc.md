# Doc

A document is read by a person or an AI who comes to it for a purpose, with what they have beside
it. Reading it once from the top, they achieve that purpose in a short time. This checklist is used
for every document, beside the checklist of the document's own kind, which holds what only that
kind needs.

A document serves its reader by what they meet in the order they read: on opening it, whether it
serves their purpose; reading on, each word and idea understood where they meet it, and each part
one they need; at the end, every question they came with answered.

## Format

```markdown
# <Title>

<The main point: what the reader can do with the document>

## <A section, in the order the reader needs it>

<The section's main point first>

- <An item said in one sentence that is understood alone>

  <Its explanation, after a blank line, indented under the item>
```

A kind's own template sets its sections; this shape holds inside it. No item is set in bold, and an
item that needs no explanation is the one sentence alone.

## Questions

### Q-1: Whether it serves their purpose

On opening the document, can the reader tell whether it serves their purpose?

Why: a reader who cannot tell reads on to find out, or leaves a document that would have served
them; one who can reads each part knowing what it supports.

### Q-2: Who does what, at a glance

Where the document tells of several that act in turn, can the reader see who does what, what passes
between them and in what order at a glance?

Why: pieced together in the head from sentences spread through the document, it costs effort and
goes wrong.

### Q-3: Every term known

Can the reader tell what each term means where they meet it, because they already know it or the
place where it is first used shows what it means?

Why: a term never explained is read with a guess at its meaning, and the reader's picture differs
from the writer's where the guess is wrong.

### Q-4: One name for each thing

Does the reader meet each thing under one name from start to end?

Why: a reader takes a second name for a second thing.

### Q-5: One meaning for each name

Does each name keep one meaning for the reader from start to end?

Why: a name whose meaning shifts carries its first meaning into the place where it has changed.

### Q-6: Read once from the top

Can the reader read each sentence without going back or ahead, because each reason and step it
rests on comes before it?

Why: a reader sent back or ahead, or reading on with a wrong guess, does not take in the picture
one reading from the top would give.

### Q-7: The form that fits

Is each thing shown in the form its reader takes in most easily for that thing?

Why: a thing put in a form that does not fit it makes the reader turn it into one that does before
they can use it.

### Q-8: Every part used

Does the reader use each part and each word toward the purpose they came for?

Why: a part the reader skips or reads twice costs effort spent on how the document is written
instead of on its subject, and buries the parts they need.

### Q-9: Each idea taken in whole

Can the reader take in each idea the document takes up whole, without guessing any part of it?

Why: an idea touched on in part is completed by a guess, and a gap covered by smooth sentences is
acted on without the reader noticing.

### Q-10: Still true later

Can the reader rely on what the document says for as long as its subject's purpose and decisions
stand?

Why: a document that says how the work went or where it stands turns wrong as the work goes on,
and is still believed.

### Q-11: Said in one place

Is each thing the document says said in one place, with every case where it does not hold beside
it?

Why: two places that say one thing come to disagree as one of them changes, and a rule read apart
from the case where it does not hold is followed there too.

### Q-12: Every statement true together

Can the reader take every statement of the document as true at once?

Why: of two statements that cannot both hold, whoever follows the document follows one and breaks
the other.
