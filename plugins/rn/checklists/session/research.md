# Research

A research result is a file in the session's folder `.rn/<yyyymmdd>-<slug>/`, where rn keeps one
piece of work. It is used by the user deciding a point it bears on, and by the maker of each later
task's result it is handed to by name, to rest their decision or their work on checked facts instead
of a guess.

A research result answers the one question a gap in the work asks, in the terms of the point or the
work it bears on. Each fact rests on a source the reader can open, current and official first, such
as the maker's own documents, a standard or a project's release notes, before what others write
about them. What the sources do not settle is said as unknown, so the reader acts on what is known
and settles the rest by asking the user or by a trial. A research result has no set shape beyond
this, so this checklist has no Format.

## Questions

### Q-1: The answer, for what it is used

From the opening, can the reader tell what the research was asked, for which point or work, and its
answer in the terms of that point or work?

Why: the user decides from the answer and the maker builds on it. An answer the reader must piece
together from the findings is drawn by each reader their own way. An answer such as which option it
favors and why, or what a later task must build on, is one they act on as it stands.

### Q-2: An answer the findings bear out

Do the findings bear out the answer, and no more than it says?

Why: an answer wider than the findings is acted on where nothing checked it.

### Q-3: Each fact found at its source

Can the reader open the source of each fact the answer rests on, and find the fact there as it is
said?

Why: a reader who cannot check a fact either does the research again or takes it on trust, and a
fact its source never said is acted on as if it had.

### Q-4: The best source for each fact

Does each fact come from the most direct and current source there is, such as a tool's own manual
for its latest release?

Why: a decision or a result resting on a fact that is stale or second-hand is made again once the
fact shows wrong. A fact that changes with a version or a date, such as a library's current
release, shows which one it holds for only with the version or the date beside it.

### Q-5: What is not known, said

Can the reader tell what the research did not settle, and what would settle it, such as a point for
the user or a trial?

Why: an unknown read as known is built on without anyone checking it, and shows only once the work
that rests on it fails. A guess, a fact of one case, or a point where sources disagree is read as
settled unless it is said to be one.
