# Usability

A deliverable, a document or code, is used by a reader who comes to it for a purpose, with what
they have beside it; the reader may be a person or an AI. Reading it once from the top, they
achieve that purpose in a short time. Below, a document is either, and each file of code is one of
its parts.

A deliverable serves its reader by what they meet in the order they read: on opening it, whether it
serves their purpose; reading on, each word and idea understood where they meet it, and each part
one they need; and at the end, every question they came with answered. Each item is answered by
what the reader has and does with the deliverable alone, never by whether it matches the sources it
was made from, which is not what the reader uses it for.

## Format

### F-1: Shape

Scope: whole

Does the document follow this shape, inside the template its own kind gives?

```markdown
# <Title>

<The main point: what the reader can do with the document>

## <A section, in the order the reader needs it>

<The section's main point first>

- <An item said in one sentence that is understood alone>

  <Its explanation, after a blank line, indented under the item>
```

No item is set in bold, and an item that needs no explanation is the one sentence alone.

Why: the reader sees on opening it whether the document serves their purpose, reads the sections in
the order they need them, and skims the items alone, reading an explanation only where they need it;
bold marks where to stop, and on every item it marks nothing out.

## Benefit

### B-1: Whether it serves their purpose

Scope: its part

On opening the document, can the reader tell whether it serves their purpose?

Why: a reader who cannot tell reads on to find out, or leaves a document that would have served
them; one who can reads each part knowing what it supports.

### B-2: Who does what, at a glance

Scope: its part

Where the document tells of several that act in turn, can the reader see who does what, what passes
between them and in what order at a glance, instead of piecing it together from sentences spread
through the document? A document that tells of no such thing passes.

Signs:
- a reader who is a person sees it in one figure, with no text beside it saying again what the
  figure shows; an AI reading a prompt follows it as steps;
- in a figure whose boxes are of different kinds, the reader finds each box by the name the text
  calls it by, with its kind beside it as one short word in angle brackets, such as
  `turn:up <agent>`.

Why: pieced together in the head, it costs effort and goes wrong; a figure shows a person at once
what rows of a table make them assemble, while an AI reads a figure as text, in order; a box that
holds a description buries the name the text calls it by.

### B-3: Every term known

Scope: each part

Can the reader tell what each term, any word the document uses for a thing of its subject, set in
code or not, means where they meet it, because they already know it or the place where it is first
used, a sentence, an example or a figure, shows what it means?

Signs: the reader learns each plain word used in a sense of its own, such as a word for a part of
the subject or for someone who acts in it, from a sentence that explains it; each name and example
the reader meets is of the subject itself or one they know, not taken from another product beside
it, such as another plugin of the same marketplace.

Why: a term never explained is read with a guess at its meaning, and the reader's picture differs
from the writer's where the guess is wrong.

### B-4: One name for each thing

Scope: whole

Does the reader meet each thing under one name from start to end?

Why: a reader takes a second name for a second thing.

### B-5: One meaning for each name

Scope: whole

Does each name keep one meaning for the reader from start to end?

Why: a name whose meaning shifts carries its first meaning into the place where it has changed.

### B-6: Read once from the top

Scope: each part

Can the reader read each sentence without going back or ahead, because each reason and step it
rests on comes before it?

Signs: the reader meets the reason for each decision before it or with it; what a sentence rests
on is already in front of the reader, not behind a link or a pointer that sends them ahead.

Why: each of these stops the reader, sends them back or ahead, or lets them read on with a wrong
guess, so the picture is not taken in by one reading from the top.

### B-7: The form that fits

Scope: each part

Is each thing shown in the form its reader takes in most easily for that thing, such as a directory
tree for where files are placed, a table for values set side by side, and sentences for a reason?

Why: a thing put in a form that does not fit it, such as files drawn as boxes, makes the reader
turn it back into the shape they know before they can use it.

### B-8: Every part and word used

Scope: each part

Does the reader use each part toward the purpose they came for, without skipping it or reading
again what they already took in, each word of it giving them something they would lose without
it?

Signs: every part is for the reader, not for someone else, such as a reviewer; the opening and
each section tell the subject, not what the document is or does; each term is met with the content
it serves, not gathered ahead of it; each comparison or example carries an idea the reader does not
already hold; each word, taken out, leaves its sentence saying less.

Why: a part the reader skips or reads twice, and every word beyond what the subject asks, costs
effort spent on how the document is written instead of on the subject, and buries the parts they
need.

### B-9: Each idea taken in whole

Scope: whole

Can the reader take in each idea the document takes up whole, without guessing any part of it?

Why: an idea touched on in part is completed by a guess, and a gap covered by smooth sentences is
acted on without the reader noticing.

### B-10: Still true later

Scope: each part

Can the reader rely on what the document says for as long as the subject's purpose and decisions
stand?

Signs: what the reader reads is the purpose, the decisions and their reasons, which stay, not
steps, status, or how the work went or a decision was reached, which change with each piece of
work.

Why: a document that has turned wrong is worse than none, since it is believed.

### B-11: Said in one place

Scope: whole

Can the reader take each thing the document says as the one account of it, because it is said in
one place, with every case where it does not hold beside it, and not also in another place of the
document?

Signs: each thing is said in one part, and every other part that needs it points there, not
saying it again in other words or in a figure.

Why: two places that say one thing come to disagree as one of them changes, and the reader cannot
tell which holds; once the reader skims a run of repeats, they skim the one new point among them
too; a rule read apart from the case where it does not hold is followed there too.

### B-12: Every statement true together

Scope: whole

Can the reader take every statement of the document as true at once, no two of them saying what
cannot both hold?

Signs: the parts that speak of one thing ask or give the same, so what one part hands to a reader
no other part keeps from it.

Why: the reader cannot tell which of the two holds, and whoever follows the document follows one
and breaks the other.
