# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `lay.py print` shows the lanes your AI proposes, in one JSON file in the form docs/lanes.md gives, with the routes of your skill and its step as it reads once laid, each lane under the action it watches with its type, the commands each subagent with a run_only lane may run and each of its steps that names in backticks a command it may not, each adapter run on its cases, and each step as it reads once laid. Once you approve them, `lay.py write` writes them from the same file: each lane's sentence at the end of its step, the lanes in `hooks/lanes.json`, one check of your plugin for each event of Claude Code's hooks, your other hooks left as they were, the adapters in `hooks/adapters/`, and Lane's code in `hooks/lane/`. After that, `claude plugin validate` runs, and `lay.py write` removes the proposal and its adapters—what runs is what you approved, and no one copies anything by hand.
- Laid again, a step loses the sentence of each lane no longer laid on it, in whichever words Lane laid it, and has a sentence in words Lane laid before put in its current words, and Lane takes out the checks of a subagent your plugin no longer has—what your subagent reads in its step is always what its checks hold it to.
- The run_only lane stops a subagent running any command but those its step names, each matched whole, and gives it `<plugin>: Run only <commands>.` with the commands it may run on the next line—the subagent goes back to its step instead of looking for another way round the refusal.
- The write_only lane stops a subagent writing any file but the outputs its step names, by the path that Write, Edit, or NotebookEdit gets, and gives it `<plugin>: Write only <output>.`—the rest of your project stays as it was. A path a lane takes names a file from the project's folder, `CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR`, however a `cd` has moved the session.
- The read_only lane stops a subagent reading any file but the inputs its step names, by the path that Read or Grep gets, and gives it `<plugin>: Read only <input>.`, letting it read the form its in_form lane names and its own part—it works from what its step gives it, not from what it happened to find.
- The read_never lane stops a subagent reading any of the files its step names, or a file under a folder it names, and gives it `<plugin>: Never read <input>.`—one role stays out of a file another keeps, such as a design tree.
- The in_form lane holds a write of an output its step names to the form the step names, a JSON Schema of the keywords Anthropic's structured outputs accept or a Markdown template's headings and frontmatter keys, before the write happens and again as the subagent gives its result, and gives it `<plugin>: Write <output> in the form of <form>.` with the point that's out on the next line—the next step doesn't meet a file out of its form.
- The edit_only lane lets a Write make an output its step names while it isn't there, and once it's there, stops a Write or NotebookEdit of it and gives the subagent `<plugin>: Once <output> is made, change it only with Edit.` Claude Code lets an Edit go only after a Read of the file—a subagent reads each part it changes, as in a design tree whose nesting holds what each part serves.
- The hold lane answers Claude Code to ask the person before a command its step names runs, with `<plugin>: Run <commands> only with the person's approval.`, so Claude Code's own permission prompt puts it to them, even under `bypassPermissions`. In `claude -p`, Claude Code has no one to ask and refuses the command—nothing you must approve runs without you, and Lane approves nothing in your place.
- Lane holds a step whose trace a machine can observe, a file read to its end by read_whole, a file made by write_step, or a command run by run_step, in the order of its step list. It stops a call that's a later step's trace while an earlier one's hasn't come back, with the step due and what that step still lacks, and it stops the result command in the same way while any step's trace hasn't come back—no step gets skipped or taken after the one that rests on it, and Lane reads no file to judge whether it went well.
- A subagent's steps may come through a steps adapter, `steps_adapter`, such as the practices of each intent it carries out read from your best practice given the start's values. Lane holds each list in its order, the subagent gets them as it starts where its prompt has come back, and two lists have no order between them—a best practice made on a run gets the same hold. A subagent held to run_only may run a command that a step list the adapter gives names.
- A sentence that no step says, as where a subagent's part holds no numbered list, comes with the part as the subagent starts.
- Lane checks the test_after, end_when, and every_item lanes as the subagent runs its result command, from the calls it kept. An output changed with no test passed after, an output not made, or an item of an input, read at the items port, whose adapter gets the start's values, whose ID stands in no file it wrote in this start after its last change of the input, stops the command with each lane's sentence and what's missing on the next line. A subagent with one of them and no route gives the result `done`—work handed on is whole, read through, tested, and answered.
- Routes, written under the proposal's `routes` as docs/routes.md gives them, start from a skill of your plugin, and Lane lays them on its step. A run of it begins as you type its command, `/<plugin>:<skill>`, or as an AI calls it, and ends once a route starts no more. Within it, Lane gives the main conversation the first start as the run begins and the next start as each subagent's end reaches it, and has it wait as each start comes back, a start counting once its subagent, run in the background, has ended. Lane stops any other start with the start due. It keeps the end of a turn going once while a start is due and then lets it end, and shows you what's left and whose step to fix, as it does where the person explicitly asks to leave a start out, Lane holding the order against drift, not against the person. It keeps a subagent about to end without a result going once with the sentence of its last step—the order of your subagents follows your routes with no script of yours, in `claude -p` and in an interactive session alike.
- A route may wait for the user, `"waits": true` in place of a `to`, for a result such as `asks` with which a subagent ends to put a point to the person. The main conversation then gets ``<plugin>: <name> gave `<on>`. Put its point to the user as it wrote it, in your reply, and resume `<plugin>:<name>` with their answer by SendMessage.`` The run goes on with no start due, Lane stops any start meanwhile with that line and lets the end of a turn go, and the resumed subagent's later result moves the routes on. Whether you type the skill again meanwhile or an AI calls it, that call ends the run and begins another. Only a route with neither a `to` nor `waits` ends the run, and only its `on` is a result the skill gives. `lay.py print` refuses a route with both, and one that waits from a subagent in `fan_out`—a role that asks the person keeps its run, and Lane holds every start after the answer.
- Routes may name your plugin's `command` that gives each start its text. Lane has the main conversation run it with the subagent's name and start the subagent with what it prints, and stops a start with any other prompt with the text to start it with—the start gets what your plugin gives it, and no AI rewords it on the way.
- A subagent whose route waits resumes only with the line `The person's answer, word for word:` and the person's own prompts since its point, which Lane keeps as the person writes them and gives the main conversation as the one message to send. Lane stops any other message to a start of the run—your subagent works from what the person said, not from a summary of it.
- Routes may leave out `step` where your skill's part holds no numbered list. Lane then lays nothing in the skill, and the run gets the routes' sentence and the skill's result command as it begins.
- Outside a run of your skill, Lane checks nothing in the main conversation, and within it only the starts of subagents, a message that resumes one, a dialog that asks the person while a subagent's point waits, which it stops so the point goes in the reply as the subagent wrote it, and the end of a turn—Lane never stops the work you do yourself in the same conversation.
- Every skill the routes name ends its run by running its result script with the result of the route that starts no more, a sentence `lay.py write` lays on the skill's last step—a plugin that calls your skill routes by that result.
- Lane stops a subagent with lanes, or that your routes name, as it starts a subagent of your plugin, or one or a dependency's skill your routes name, and says to it that only the main conversation starts one—each start gets what your plugin gives it, not what another subagent chose to pass on.
- A subagent with no run_only lane runs any command, and Lane stops only a run of its result script on a result it may not give—a reviewer reads the commits with no rule written for it.
- A route may name a skill of a dependency, `<dependency>:<skill>`, called by the main conversation as one move that ends as the dependency's result script runs there with a result a route is on, your plugin's checks holding nothing meanwhile—a plugin's whole run is one step of yours, held by its own checks.
- A subagent gives its result by running the result script Lane puts into your plugin, `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/hooks/lane/result.py <subagent> <result>`, which exits with an error naming the results it may give where no route is on it, and Lane keeps the result only from a run that succeeded—Lane never reads a reply such as "No more entries" as `more`.
- A lane may take a thing given for each start of a subagent, written `{<name>}`, the value under that name read at the values port from the prompt of the Agent call that started the subagent and each message a SendMessage call sent to it after, the later over the earlier, the prompt kept under the `agentId` the call gives back, so Lane holds each of the starts of one subagent at once to their own. A value given as `null` lets the lane through on that start, but a command a lane takes holding it names no command—one sentence holds a subagent to the files of each start, a file added by a message held from then on, and a start given nothing to run runs nothing.
- `lay.py print` refuses a subagent with lanes, or named by your routes, whose file doesn't set `background: true`, and the check stops each call of one whose file no longer does—every start goes in the background, your conversation stays free, starts that stand apart go at once, and the ID each gives back sets it apart from the others.
- A run_only, hold, or test_after lane may name a command of a dependency's skill, writing the folder Claude Code installed the dependency in as `${dependency:<name>}`. That part stands for any folder holding the manifest of the plugin of that name, which your plugin's own manifest declares in its `dependencies`, and the rest must match whole—your subagent runs the scripts of the plugins yours depends on, and nothing else in their place.
- Routes may name in `fan_out` the subagents your skill may start several times at once, each start with its own prompt and values. Lane takes the route once every start has ended, from the result they share or, where they differ, the first of them in the order your routes give in `precedence`, which `lay.py print` asks of each subagent fanned out with more than one result—work split over starts at once goes on by one result, by your plugin's own order.
- A run_only, hold, or test_after command may hold a value given for the start as one part, such as `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/check.py {file}`, the rest matched whole and the value put in as one word of the shell, quoted where it holds a blank or another character of the shell—a command runs on each start's own file, and no value adds a word to it.
- A path of a read_only, write_only, or read_whole lane may begin with `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`, and Lane puts your plugin's folder in for it, as in a command—a step holds its subagent to a file of your plugin, such as how it should work, by the path it reads it by.
- Where your plugin writes a text in another form than Lane reads, an adapter of yours, a Python 3 script with the cases it must give, comes with the proposal for your approval, runs on its cases before Lane prints it, and runs in the check within its time—Lane holds your plugin in the form it writes in, with no lane made for it.
- A subagent whose entry names its part, one Markdown file under your plugin's `domain/`, as the plugin best practice keeps the work's knowledge, has its steps read from the part, shown by `lay.py print`, and laid there by `lay.py write`, and gets the part whole as it starts, your plugin's folder put in—your subagent has its steps before its first call, and subagents sharing a part share one set of sentences.
- Lane stops what it can't check, or keeps the subagent going once as it ends, and gives it and you `<plugin>: lane cannot check <lane> on step <n> of <subagent>: <what is wrong>.` with who must fix it, such as a value not given, an adapter failing, a step no longer saying its sentence, or a form gone—nothing passes unchecked, and you know who owns the fix.
- Without Python 3.9 or later run as `python3`, Lane stops each tool call of a subagent with lanes, and asks the subagent and you to install it—Lane never leaves a lane unkept with no one told.
- `lay.py up` brings an older copy of Lane's code in your plugin and its checks up to Lane's version, puts each sentence a step says in words Lane laid before in Lane's current words, the rest of the step as it was, and says each lane and adapter laid before, for your AI to keep in its proposal—your lanes keep working across Lane's updates, and none you approved goes missing unsaid.
