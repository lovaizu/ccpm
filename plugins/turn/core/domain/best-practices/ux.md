# ux

## Goal

The developer builds what the product's users want.

## User benefits

- **Q1** (Roles, OKRs): Does the developer find each one who comes to the product with a want of their own, and an objective for that want, so they leave out nothing the product's users want?
- **Q2** (Roles, OKRs): Reading an objective, does the developer know which role it is for and what that role gets from the product?
- **Q3** (OKRs): Weighing a piece of work, can the developer tell which one objective it brings closer, or that it brings none closer?
- **Q4** (OKRs): After a change, can the developer tell from the key results whether to keep it?
- **Q5** (Roles, OKRs, Scenarios): For each objective, can the developer follow, exchange by exchange, how its role goes from asking the product to having what the objective says?
- **Q6** (the whole): Can the developer change how the product works inside, while its roles see the same, and leave the UX as it is?

## Practices

### Q1: Does the developer find each one who comes to the product with a want of their own, and an objective for that want, so they leave out nothing the product's users want?

**Thinking**: Roles are cut by the want each brings to the product, not by what kind of thing takes them, and a want with no objective is never built.

**Steps**

1. List each one who comes to the product with a want of their own, by the role they play, such as a guest, a member or an admin.
2. Merge roles that want the same, and give each role left the objective of its want.

**Grounds**

- [Writing Effective Use Cases](https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704id_/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf), Cockburn, draft in preparation for Addison-Wesley, 1999, The one page Summary: "Brainstorm all the actors, human and non, who have an operational goal against the system."
- [Define Your Market](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done-playbook/define-your-market/), Ulwick, Jobs-to-be-Done Playbook, strategyn.com, 2021: "Products don’t have jobs-to-be-done—people do."
- [Barbara Minto: “MECE: I invented it, so I get to say how to pronounce it”](https://karriere.mckinsey.de/alumni/news-and-events/global-news/alumni-news/barbara-minto-mece-i-invented-it-so-i-get-to-say-how-to-pronounce-it), McKinsey & Company, alumni news, 2018: "The concept required the groups of ideas to be MECE—divided pieces that were mutually exclusive of each other and collectively exhaustive in terms of the whole."

### Q2: Reading an objective, does the developer know which role it is for and what that role gets from the product?

**Thinking**: An objective is the progress one role is trying to make, said from where they stand, not what the product does.

**Steps**

1. Write each objective as one sentence that fits on one line: the role as its subject, then a verb, its object and the context it is done in, with no feature or mechanism of the product.
2. Ask whether the product could do all the sentence says while the role is no better off. If so, rewrite the sentence around what the role gains.

**Grounds**

- [Jobs to Be Done Theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), Clayton Christensen Institute, christenseninstitute.org, 2024: "Everyone has Jobs to Be Done in their lives—the progress they’re trying to make as they strive toward a goal or aspiration within particular circumstances."
- [Jobs to Be Done (JTBD): The Original Framework by Tony Ulwick](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/), Strategyn (Tiffany Anderson), strategyn.com, 2024: "Identify who is executing the job and define the core functional job in a single, solution-free statement."
- [Define Your Market](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done-playbook/define-your-market/), Ulwick, Jobs-to-be-Done Playbook, strategyn.com, 2021: "The product will help the group of people: [verb] + [object] + [contextual clarifier]"
- [Google's OKR Playbook](https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook), Google, whatmatters.com, no date: "Could the OKR get a 1.0 under reasonable circumstances — without providing direct enduser or economic benefit? If so, then reword the OKR to focus on the tangible benefit."
- [Google's OKR Playbook](https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook), Google, whatmatters.com, no date: "If your Objective doesn’t fit on one line, it probably isn’t crisp enough."

### Q3: Weighing a piece of work, can the developer tell which one objective it brings closer, or that it brings none closer?

**Thinking**: The developer cuts objectives so that each piece of work brings one of them closer, and no gain appears twice.

**Steps**

1. Merge objectives that one piece of work would bring closer together. Split an objective that holds gains a piece of work could bring closer apart. Go on until each piece of work brings exactly one closer.
2. Take each change planned or recently made that alters what a role gets, and name the one objective it brings closer.

**Grounds**

- [Set goals with OKRs](https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs), Google re:Work, rework.withgoogle.com, 2016: "Once the organization knows what it’s focused on and how it will measure success, it can become easier for individuals to connect their projects with the organizational objectives."
- [Set goals with OKRs](https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs), Google re:Work, rework.withgoogle.com, 2016: "Do the team priorities connect to any of the organizational key results?"

### Q4: After a change, can the developer tell from the key results whether to keep it?

**Thinking**: Each objective has two key results that count what its role does or feels: KR.1 how well the role gets what the objective says, and KR.2 the lead time to having it, which may get shorter only while KR.1 does not get worse, as a result had fast but not well is no gain.

**Steps**

1. List how success or failure would show in what the role does or feels, such as giving up, undoing a result or working around the product.
2. Turn the signs into KR.1 and KR.2. Make each a share, an average per user, a count or a time, and name what it is counted out of.
3. Ask whether both key results could get better while the role still does not get what the objective says. If so, rework them until they could not.

**Grounds**

What actions would indicate the goal had been met?"
- [Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf), Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, CHI 2010: "Sometimes failure is easier to identify than success"
- [Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf), Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, CHI 2010: "Choose signals that are sensitive and specific to the goal – they should move only when the user experience is better or worse, not for other, unrelated reasons."

### Q5: For each objective, can the developer follow, exchange by exchange, how its role goes from asking the product to having what the objective says?

**Thinking**: A scenario is one run of the main path to the objective, the one most of its role take, from the request that starts it to the gain that ends it, each exchange between those Roles names, the product and those outside it.

**Steps**

1. Name each scenario by its objective's ID and a short active verb phrase saying how the role reaches it.
2. Write each exchange as one message, from the one who takes it to the one it acts on, saying what it passes, checks or changes, such as an arrow in a Mermaid sequence diagram, and mark a part that repeats as a loop with its condition.
3. Read the scenario from start to end: the first message is the role's request, the last gives the role the end, and each moves toward it.

**Grounds**

- [Basic Use Case Template](https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073041/http://alistair.cockburn.us/Basic+use+case+template), Cockburn, HaT TR.96.03a, 1998: "<put here the steps of the scenario from trigger to goal delivery, and any cleanup after>"
- [Basic Use Case Template](https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073041/http://alistair.cockburn.us/Basic+use+case+template), Cockburn, HaT TR.96.03a, 1998: "Trigger: <the action upon the system that starts the use case, may be time event>"

### Q6: Can the developer change how the product works inside, while its roles see the same, and leave the UX as it is?

**Thinking**: The product is one closed box: the UX says only what its roles mean to do, see and receive, so any design that gives them the same leaves it true.

**Steps**

1. Pick one change the design could make while the roles see the same, read every line, and flag each line that would have to change.
2. Rewrite each flagged line by what the role does, sees or receives, or leave it to the document that describes the design inside, such as the architecture.

**Grounds**

- [Writing Effective Use Cases](https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704id_/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf), Cockburn, draft in preparation for Addison-Wesley, 1999: "For computer systems, it is rare that we open up the black box."
- [Writing Effective Use Cases](https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704id_/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf), Cockburn, draft in preparation for Addison-Wesley, 1999: "Show the intent of the actor, what it/they want and get accomplished in that step."
- [Deriving Specifications from Requirements: An Example](https://www.pamelazave.com/turnstile.pdf), Jackson and Zave, ICSE 1995: "The requirement is concerned entirely with the environment, where the effects and benefits of the machine will be felt and assessed: the machine is purely a means to the end of achieving the required effect in the environment."
