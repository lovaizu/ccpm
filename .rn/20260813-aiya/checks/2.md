### NG-1 — "dispatch round" is used once and defined nowhere

- **Claim**: design.md:209 "Between gates the Conductor self-runs within a dispatch round — re-planning
  included; across rounds the loop re-enters from file state."
- **Reality**: this is the term's only occurrence in the document set (the "round" at design.md:392 and
  :418 is a different, plain-English use — one generate-verify cycle). Nothing says what delimits a
  dispatch round or who starts the next one.
- **Consequence**: the sentence that carries the task-#1 NG-5 fix — the design's answer to "who keeps
  the loop moving" — rests on a term the reader cannot resolve from the design alone.
- **Proposed fix**: define it in the same sentence or the one before: a dispatch round is one
  continuous run of the Conductor between two stops (a gate, or `dn`) — within it the Conductor keeps
  dispatching and re-planning on its own; a round ends when the Conductor stops and waits, and the next
  one is entered from file state. Rationale: it names exactly the platform boundary task-#1 NG-5
  established (self-running holds within one turn's tool-calling loop). Trade-off: one more sentence of
  platform coupling in §4.4; the alternative — dropping the term for "between stops" — loses the handle
  §4.5 needs for "across rounds".

### NG-3 — the design's command vocabulary misaligns with the decided one

- **Claim**: design.md:63 "The human replies with two words — `/ty` (approve) and `/gm` (send back)";
  :211 "`/gm`"; :217 and :422 "`dn` → `/clear` → `up`".
- **Reality**: the decided vocabulary is `/aiya:ty`, `/aiya:gm`, `/aiya:on`, `/aiya:dn`, `/aiya:up`
  (README.md:14; namespacing is a platform fact per checks/1.md OK-8), with bare words `on dn up ty gm`
  as the README's shorthand (README.md:158-164). `/ty` and `/gm` exist in neither form — a user typing
  `/ty` gets nothing. `dn` and `up` appear in the design without ever being introduced as the plugin's
  suspend/resume commands, and `on` never appears at all.
- **Consequence**: the design's only user-facing words disagree with the UX document that owns them
  (references/ux.md:18-19: every user-touched name is decided in the UX vocabulary, "never let a name
  be born during implementation").
- **Proposed fix**: use the bare-word shorthand consistently (`ty`, `gm`, `dn`, `up`), and at first use
  (:63) one clause anchoring them: "the plugin's command words — the full forms and the fifth word,
  `on`, are the README's vocabulary". Rationale: bare words match the README's own shorthand and stay
  stable if the namespace ever changes. Trade-off: none of substance; the alternative (full `/aiya:ty`
  everywhere) is heavier and duplicates UX detail into the design.

### NG-4 — "exactly one Turn(generate)" contradicts re-aim and §6's own counting

- **Claim**: design.md:59 "a **Step** is one Plan item, whose work is carried by exactly one
  Turn(generate)."
- **Reality**: a re-aimed Step is redispatched to a fresh Turn(generate) up to three times
  (design.md:157), and §6 counts accordingly — keep rate is "Steps ÷ Turn(generate)s (= Steps +
  re-aims)" (design.md:396), i.e. one Step can consume several Turn(generate)s. :142 states the intended
  reading ("Count by 1 Step = 1 Turn(generate)") as a counting convention, not a cardinality.
- **Consequence**: the vocabulary-fixing sentence of §3.1 is false in every re-aim case the rest of the
  design depends on.
- **Proposed fix**: ":59 … carried by one Turn(generate) per attempt" (or "at a time"). Rationale: it
  preserves the point being made — a Step is never spread across concurrent Turns — while agreeing with
  :157 and :396. Trade-off: none.

### NG-5 — "formats for every product are in references/" does not hold for Research

### NG-7 — what the plugin is physically made of is never enumerated

- **Claim** (completion criterion 1): a reader can answer from the design alone "what the plugin is
  made of."
- **Reality**: the design names the pieces separately — Turns are subagents from three static
  definitions with pinned tools (design.md:29, :41, :61, :233), no controller program is written and
  "the loop is carried by procedural prose" (:43), the commands `dn`/`up` exist (:217) — but never
  states the composition: that the Conductor *is* the main conversation following that procedural
  prose, that the five command words are the plugin's skills, and that the three Turn definitions are
  the plugin's agent files. checks/1.md NG-3/NG-5 reasoned from exactly this composition; the design
  does not record it.
- **Consequence**: the one structural question the criterion names — what is this thing, on the
  platform it runs on — is answerable only by inference.
- **Proposed fix**: three sentences, at the end of §2 (:43, where the platform facts already live) or in
  §3.5: the plugin = five skills (the command words) + three agent definitions (the Turns) + the
  references/ formats; the Conductor is the main session executing the skills' procedural prose; there
  is no other executable part. Rationale: :43 already commits the design to platform facts, so naming
  the composition is completing that paragraph, not adding coupling. Trade-off: the design becomes
  Claude-Code-specific in one more place — but §5.1's "spawn tool absent from the definition" already
  crossed that line.

### NG-8 — the walkthrough shows five stops, not six

- **Promise**: "six checkpoints" (README.md:3, :152); the design has the Conductor stop and wait at
  every gate (design.md:209), and the Approach phase's Planning Gate reviews "the Steps that will
  produce the Approach" (design.md:206).
- **Reality**: the walkthrough stops at the purpose plan (:43-48), G1 (:67), G2 (:85), the delivery
  plan (:96-100), and G3 (:120-123) — five. Between G1's approval and G2, the approach investigations
  start with no plan confirmation: README.md:78 "After your `/aiya:ty`, the investigations that decide
  the approach run. Again, you just wait", and the :81-83 console block carries no 👉 prompt.
- **Consequence**: the README contradicts itself (six promised, five shown) and the design (a gate at
  which no one stops is not a gate); a reader counting the example's checkpoints gets the wrong model.
- **Proposed fix**: insert the Approach Planning Gate stop before :81 — a two-line console block in the
  :43 pattern ("👉 Confirm the plan for settling the approach — 3 investigations … /aiya:ty"), and make
  :78's `ty` refer to it. Rationale: the six-gate structure is the decided experience; the walkthrough
  is what teaches it. Trade-off: one more beat in the walkthrough. The alternative — declaring this
  gate skippable — would change the decided six-gate experience and is not proposed.

### NG-9 — three console behaviors the README promises have no carrier in the design

- **Promise**, item by item:
  - `/aiya:gm` with no argument picks up the review comments on the PR (README.md:150).
  - Progress is always visible, mid-run, outside gates (README.md:105-113 — the Wave-2 status board).
  - `dn` commits and pushes progress (README.md:132 "Progress committed and pushed").
- **Reality**: the design defines `gm` only as "send back" (design.md:63) and re-approval versioning
  (:211) — argument-less behavior is nowhere; the console appears only as the gate-summary surface
  (:209); §4.5 (:215-217) defines the resume point and the cycling but never says `dn` commits or
  pushes anything.
- **Consequence**: three behaviors of the decided experience exist only in the README; the design is
  silent, so an implementer working from the design alone would not build them.
- **Proposed fix**: one sentence each — §4.4: with no argument, `gm` takes the PR's review comments as
  the send-back feedback; §4.4: between gates the Conductor keeps a progress board current on the
  console (waves, running items, attempts); §4.5: `dn` commits and pushes the run records, so the
  resume point survives the machine as well as the conversation. Rationale: the completion criterion
  makes the design the carrier of every promised behavior. Trade-off: the design gains UX-level detail;
  the alternative — ruling the README (as the UX document) the owner of these three and the design
  deliberately silent — is coherent but needs the user to rule it explicitly.

### NG-10 — "decided by their consumes lines alone" overrides the same-file exception

- **Promise**: README.md:103 "whether items may be bundled is decided by their consumes lines alone."
- **Reality**: both the design and the format state one exception — "Two items writing the same file
  are dependent, whatever is written" (design.md:357; references/plan.md:13 "One exception…must not be
  bundled").
- **Consequence**: "alone" teaches a bundling rule that, followed literally, produces exactly the
  corrupted-wave failure §7 warns about (design.md:420).
- **Proposed fix**: drop "alone" or fold the guard in: "…decided by their consumes lines — plus one
  guard: two items that write the same file are never bundled." Rationale: the callout is the README's
  only statement of the bundling rule, so it must carry the exception. Trade-off: none.
