# Research: ux

Sources for the best practice of ux: how to write a UX document, `dev/<plugin>/ux.md`.
Its user is whoever builds or changes the product, a person or an AI.
Every quote below was copied from the page at its URL, opened on 2026-11-02.
I read each PDF through a text extractor I wrote for this, so I compared the words and their order; where it split a word or dropped a ligature, such as "user - centered" or "eects", the quote gives the word as printed.

## The objective is the progress a user is trying to make

### People, not products, have jobs to be done

- Source: Tony Ulwick, "Define Your Market", Jobs-to-be-Done Playbook, strategyn.com, published 2021-02-16.
- URL: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done-playbook/define-your-market/
- Quote:

  > Products don’t have jobs-to-be-done—people do.

- What it says: the objective's subject is the user, and the product is only how they get it.

### A job is the progress someone tries to make

- Source: Clayton Christensen Institute, "Jobs to Be Done Theory", christenseninstitute.org, published 2024-05-12.
- URL: https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/
- Quote:

  > Everyone has Jobs to Be Done in their lives—the progress they’re trying to make as they strive toward a goal or aspiration within particular circumstances.

- What it says: an objective says the progress the user is after, in the circumstances they are in.

### The job is one statement, free of the solution

- Source: Tiffany Anderson, "Jobs to Be Done (JTBD): The Original Framework by Tony Ulwick", strategyn.com, published 2024-11-11.
- URL: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/
- Quote:

  > Identify who is executing the job and define the core functional job in a single, solution-free statement.

- What it says: the objective names who, and what they get, in one sentence with no feature in it.

### A job statement is a verb, an object and its context

- Source: Tony Ulwick, "Define Your Market", Jobs-to-be-Done Playbook, strategyn.com, published 2021-02-16.
- URL: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done-playbook/define-your-market/
- Quote:

  > The product will help the group of people: [verb] + [object] + [contextual clarifier]

- What it says: this gives the objective's sentence its shape after the user.

### Every user, human or not, is found first

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > Brainstorm all the actors, human and non, who have an operational goal against the system.

- What it says: the users of a plugin may be AIs as well as people, and each with a goal of its own may need its objective.

### An objective that helps no one is reworded

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > Could the OKR get a 1.0 under reasonable circumstances — without providing direct enduser or economic benefit? If so, then reword the OKR to focus on the tangible benefit.

- What it says: the test of an objective is whether the user is better off once it is met.

### An objective fits on one line

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > If your Objective doesn’t fit on one line, it probably isn’t crisp enough.

- What it says: a sentence longer than a line holds a how or a second gain.

## A key result counts what users do as they come closer

### Signals come from user behavior and attitudes

- Source: Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", Proceedings of CHI 2010, ACM Press (venue as research.google gives it).
- URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf
- Quote:

  > Next, think about how success or failure in the goals might manifest itself in user behavior or attitudes. What actions would indicate the goal had been met?

- What it says: key results are found in what the user does or feels.

### Failure is often easier to see

- Source: Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", Proceedings of CHI 2010, ACM Press (venue as research.google gives it).
- URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf
- Quote:

  > Sometimes failure is easier to identify than success

- What it says: giving up and undoing are signs to count as well as success.

### A signal moves only with the experience

- Source: Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", Proceedings of CHI 2010, ACM Press (venue as research.google gives it).
- URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf
- Quote:

  > Choose signals that are sensitive and specific to the goal – they should move only when the user experience is better or worse, not for other, unrelated reasons.

- What it says: a count of output that can rise with no gain to users is dropped.

### Impact, not the launch

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > What matters isn’t the launch, but its impact.

- What it says: features shipped or tasks done are not key results.

### Counts are normalized

- Source: Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", Proceedings of CHI 2010, ACM Press (venue as research.google gives it).
- URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf
- Quote:

  > Raw counts will go up as your user base grows, and need to be normalized; ratios, percentages, or averages per user are often more useful.

- What it says: a key result names what it is counted out of.

### A measure says exactly what it counts

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > Make sure the metrics are unambiguous. If you say “1 million users,” is that all-time users or seven-day actives?

- What it says: the builder reads each key result one way only.

### A measure made a target stops measuring

- Source: Wikipedia, "Goodhart's law", en.wikipedia.org; the article gives no single date. It reports that Marilyn Strathern stated the law in these words in a 1997 paper in European Review; I did not open that paper.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law
- Quote:

  > Goodhart's law is an adage that has been stated as, "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure".

- What it says: a key result gives the better direction and no target value.

### All key results met must mean the objective met

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > Is it reasonably possible to score 1.0 on all the Key Results, but still not achieve the intent of the Objective? If so, add or rework the Key Results until their successful completion guarantees that the Objective is also successfully completed.

- What it says: the key results together are checked against the objective, not one by one.

## Each piece of work brings one objective closer

### No gain is counted twice, and none is left out

- Source: Wikipedia, "MECE principle", en.wikipedia.org; the article gives no single date.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MECE_principle
- Quote:

  > The principle has been used in the business mapping process wherein the optimum arrangement of information is exhaustive and does not double count at any level of the hierarchy.

- What it says: two objectives with the same gain are merged, and a gain that no objective holds is added.

### Each project connects to an objective

- Source: Google re:Work Editorial Team, "Set goals with OKRs", rework.withgoogle.com; the page says "Originally published 2016".
- URL: https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs
- Quote:

  > Once the organization knows what it’s focused on and how it will measure success, it can become easier for individuals to connect their projects with the organizational objectives.

- What it says: each change is named with the objective it brings closer.

### Work is checked against the key results above it

- Source: Google re:Work Editorial Team, "Set goals with OKRs", rework.withgoogle.com; the page says "Originally published 2016".
- URL: https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs
- Quote:

  > Do the team priorities connect to any of the organizational key results?

- What it says: a change that connects to no objective is a sign to check the objectives.

## Of two key results that move against each other, one comes first

### OKRs say what tradeoffs to make

- Source: Google, "Google's OKR Playbook", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/google-okr-playbook
- Quote:

  > Well done OKRs are a motivational management tool that helps make it clear to teams what’s important, what to optimize, and what tradeoffs to make during their day-to-day work.

- What it says: the OKRs tell the builder which key result to keep when two pull apart.

### Metrics can move in conflicting directions

- Source: Mårten Schultzberg, Sebastian Ankargren and Mattias Frånberg, "Risk-Aware Product Decisions in A/B Tests with Multiple Metrics", Spotify Engineering, engineering.atspotify.com, published 2024-03-05.
- URL: https://engineering.atspotify.com/2024/03/risk-aware-product-decisions-in-a-b-tests-with-multiple-metrics
- Quote:

  > Clearly, this rule is insufficient when, for example, metrics move in conflicting directions.

- What it says: a change can make one measure better and another worse, so the pairs that can do so are found first.

### Key results are paired, effect and counter-effect

- Source: Bruce Gil, "Pairing Quantity and Quality Key Results: What are Some Examples?", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date. It reports Andy Grove's words from High Output Management; I did not open that book.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/quantity-quality-key-results-pairing-examples
- Quote:

  > In High Output Management, Andy Grove describes pairing Key Results as measuring “both effect and counter-effect.”

- What it says: a key result that counts how much or how fast is set beside one that counts how well.

### A gain in speed can cost errors

- Source: Bruce Gil, "Pairing Quantity and Quality Key Results: What are Some Examples?", whatmatters.com; the page prints no date. It reports Andy Grove's words from High Output Management; I did not open that book.
- URL: https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/quantity-quality-key-results-pairing-examples
- Quote:

  > This OKR anticipates that reducing pit stop time can lead to cutting corners or at the very least unintentional mistakes as the team speeds up their work.

- What it says: for each pair, the builder imagines one change that makes one better and the other worse.

### Ask which goal is more important

- Source: Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", Proceedings of CHI 2010, ACM Press (venue as research.google gives it).
- URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf
- Quote:

  > Use the HEART framework to prompt articulation of goals (e.g. is it more important to attract new users, or to encourage existing users to become more engaged?).

- What it says: the builder asks which of the two matters more to what the objective says.

### The rule is written before it is used

- Source: Mårten Schultzberg, Sebastian Ankargren and Mattias Frånberg, "Risk-Aware Product Decisions in A/B Tests with Multiple Metrics", Spotify Engineering, engineering.atspotify.com, published 2024-03-05.
- URL: https://engineering.atspotify.com/2024/03/risk-aware-product-decisions-in-a-b-tests-with-multiple-metrics
- Quote:

  > By articulating the heuristics that govern your decision-making process, you can design and analyze the experiment in a way that respects how you make decisions in the end.

- What it says: the priority is written down, so each later change is judged by it.

## A scenario runs the main path from the request to the gain

### The name is the goal as an active verb phrase

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Basic Use Case Template", Humans and Technology technical report TR.96.03a, version date October 26, 1998; the author's page kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073041/http://alistair.cockburn.us/Basic+use+case+template
- Quote:

  > Use Case: <number> <the name should be the goal as a short active verb phrase>

- What it says: a scenario's heading says how the user reaches the objective, the user as its subject.

### Steps run from the trigger to the goal

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Basic Use Case Template", Humans and Technology technical report TR.96.03a, version date October 26, 1998; the author's page kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073041/http://alistair.cockburn.us/Basic+use+case+template
- Quote:

  > <put here the steps of the scenario from trigger to goal delivery, and any cleanup after>

- What it says: the diagram starts at the user's first request and ends where the user has the gain.

### The trigger starts the run

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Basic Use Case Template", Humans and Technology technical report TR.96.03a, version date October 26, 1998; the author's page kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073041/http://alistair.cockburn.us/Basic+use+case+template
- Quote:

  > Trigger: <the action upon the system that starts the use case, may be time event>

- What it says: the first message is the user's request to the product.

### The main success scenario is the common one

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > The main success scenario is the most common, or perhaps easiest to understand, scenario.

- What it says: the diagram shows the path most users take, with failures left off.

### Each step passes, checks or changes

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > Write each sentence as a goal succeeding, distinctly moving the process forward. Write: "At some time, ActorA kicks ActorB with some information", or "System verifies validation conditions are met", or "System updates its state", or "ActorA has ActorB kick ActorC".

- What it says: each message says who acts on whom, and what it passes, checks or changes.

### A repeated part is a loop

- Source: Mermaid, "Sequence diagrams", mermaid.js.org; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://mermaid.js.org/syntax/sequenceDiagram.html
- Quote:

  > It is possible to express loops in a sequence diagram.

- What it says: a step that repeats is drawn once, inside a loop with its condition.

### A run is checked from the trigger to the end

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > Does it run from trigger to delivery of the success end condition?

- What it says: the finished diagram is read from top to bottom against its start and its end.

## Each participant is named with its role and its kind

### An actor is a role, played by people or systems

- Source: Object Management Group, "OMG Unified Modeling Language (OMG UML)", version 2.5.1, December 2017, chapter 18, Use Cases.
- URL: https://www.omg.org/spec/UML/2.5.1/PDF
- Quote:

  > An Actor models a type of role played by an entity that interacts with the subjects of its associated UseCases (e.g., by exchanging signals and data). Actors may represent roles played by human users, external hardware, or other systems.

- What it says: a participant is named by its role, and its kind says what plays it.

### An actor may be a person or a program

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > An actor might be a person, a company or organization, a computer program or a computer system, hardware or software or both.

- What it says: the kinds a person, an AI and a script are all actors.

### A participant has an identifier and a label

- Source: Mermaid, "Sequence diagrams", mermaid.js.org; the page prints no date.
- URL: https://mermaid.js.org/syntax/sequenceDiagram.html
- Quote:

  > The actor can have a convenient identifier and a descriptive label.

- What it says: each diagram declares each participant with its name and kind as the label.

### It is clear who takes each step

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > Is it clear which actor is operating the goal (who is "kicking the ball?)

- What it says: every message's sender is a declared participant.

## The document says only what users meet

### The system is kept a black box

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > For computer systems, it is rare that we open up the black box.

- What it says: the scenarios name no piece of the product the user does not meet.

### A step shows the intent

- Source: Alistair Cockburn, "Writing Effective Use Cases", draft "in preparation for Addison-Wesley Longman, Q3 2000", each page marked "A.Cockburn 1999"; a PDF kept by the Internet Archive.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20210301131704/https://www.infor.uva.es/~mlaguna/is1/materiales/BookDraft1.pdf
- Quote:

  > Show the intent of the actor, what it/they want and get accomplished in that step.

- What it says: a message says what the user means and gets, not how the product does it.

### The requirement lives in the user's world

- Source: Michael Jackson and Pamela Zave, "Deriving Specifications from Requirements: An Example", 17th International Conference on Software Engineering, ACM Press, 1995; the PDF on Pamela Zave's site.
- URL: https://www.pamelazave.com/turnstile.pdf
- Quote:

  > The requirement is concerned entirely with the environment, where the effects and benefits of the machine will be felt and assessed: the machine is purely a means to the end of achieving the required effect in the environment.

- What it says: what the product must do for users is said where users are; how it does it is the architecture's.
