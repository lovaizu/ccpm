# Product vision of rn

## Roles

- user\
  One who asks rn for a deliverable, such as a report, code, or a plugin, for the people it's for.

## Objectives and key results

- O1: the user gets each deliverable they mean, one that its people can use for what they're after.
  - KR1.1: the share of the deliverables rn hands over that the people they're for couldn't use
    for what they're after. Lower is better.
  - KR1.2: the time from the user's request to the deliverable, including the user's own time
    answering rn. Shorter is better.

The first key result of each objective comes first. Keep a change that improves a key result
only if none before it gets worse.
Key results have no targets, as a measure made a target stops being a good measure, by Goodhart's
law. They show whether a change brought its objective closer.

## Concept

rn piles up the [vital few](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) that bring a
deliverable's people to their aim, and cuts the trivial many thoroughly.

- The goal reached earliest, for O1\
  Each work aims at one opportunity that the user picks, and puts in the user's hands early the
  least that runs, as a [walking skeleton](https://wiki.c2.com/?WalkingSkeleton), so what doesn't
  serve shows before rn builds it.
- The user as one who uses it, for O1\
  The user picks the opportunity and gives feedback on what runs, as its people would, and rn
  leads the way to the goal and settles how it's made.
- Best practices in full, for O1\
  Each deliverable that rn makes meets every necessary condition of its best practice, while what
  rn makes stays the least.
