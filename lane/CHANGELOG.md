# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The hands_off lane stops a subagent running a command its step says not to run, in the forms the AI writes, such as `git -C notes commit`, `cd notes && git commit` or `GIT_EDITOR=true git commit`, and tells it `<plugin>: Do not run <commands>.` — the subagent goes back to its step instead of looking for another way round the refusal.
- The start_with lane stops each call a subagent makes before it has run the script its step says to run first, the script taken as the step writes the command, such as `python3 "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/turn.py" given`, and tells it `<plugin>: First run <script>.` — the subagent begins from what the script gives it, not from what it looked for on its own.
- The read_only lane stops a subagent reading a file beside the inputs its step names, by the Read or Grep tool or by `cat`, `head`, `tail`, `sed` or `grep`, and tells it `<plugin>: Read only <input>.`, letting it read the form its in_form lane names — it works from what its step gives it, not from what it happened to find, and still reads the form its step tells it to write in.
- The read_whole lane stops a subagent writing while it has read only part of an input its step names, read from what it has done since it started, and tells it `<plugin>: Read <input> to its end.` — it does not act on the first part of a long input alone.
- The write_only lane stops a subagent writing any file but the outputs its step names, with Write, Edit or NotebookEdit, or in the folder it works in with a command, such as a redirect, `tee`, `cp`, `mv` or `sed -i`, and tells it `<plugin>: Write only <output>.` — the rest of your project stays as it was, and the subagent goes back to writing what its step names.
- The in_form lane stops a subagent writing an output its step names out of the form the step names, before the write is made: JSON, or JSONL line by line, held to a JSON Schema of the keywords Anthropic's structured outputs accept, or Markdown held to a template's headings and frontmatter keys; it tells `<plugin>: Write <output> in the form of <form>.`, the form named by a path the subagent can read, with the one point that is out on the next line, its place, what was expected and what was found — the subagent puts that point right, and the next step does not meet a file out of its form.
- The test_after lane keeps a subagent going as it is about to end when its last change to an output its step names has no passing run of the test after it, and tells it `<plugin>: After changing <output>, pass <test>.` — a change does not reach you untested; a run passes only where its exit is the test's, so `; echo exit=$?` after a failing test does not count.
- The as_printed lane stops a subagent handing on, as an Agent call's prompt or a SendMessage's message, anything but what a command its step names last printed, word for word with one heading line allowed, until it has, and keeps it going when it is about to end with a last reply that holds none of it, telling it `<plugin>: Hand on <input> as it is.` — what reaches the next agent or you is what the command printed, not the subagent's retelling of it.
- The follow_next lane stops a subagent's calls, once a script its step names has printed the next move as one JSON line `{"tool": ..., "input": {...}}`, until it takes that move, and tells it `<plugin>: Take the next move <script> printed.` — the run goes where your script sends it.
- The wait_for lane stops a subagent running a command its step holds back until a message holding the approval, such as `OK`, has reached it since it last asked, and tells it `<plugin>: Do not go on until <approval>.`; `lay.py print` shows the commands it holds back beside its hook — nothing you have to approve goes on before you have.
- The end_when lane keeps a subagent going as it is about to end when it has not made the output its step names in this run, and tells it `<plugin>: End only once <output> is made.` — a file left from an earlier run does not stand in for the work.
- The every_item lane keeps a subagent going as it is about to end when an item of an input its step names, a top-level list item keyed by an ID such as `B-1`, is named neither in its last reply nor in a file it wrote, and tells it `<plugin>: Answer every item of <input>.` — no finding is dropped unanswered.
- A lane may take a thing given for each run, written `{<name>}` in its step, such as `{work}`: the value under that name in the JSON the subagent starts from, its prompt or what the script start_with runs first prints, a list standing for each of its texts; a drift is told with the sentence as the step says it and the run's value on the next line, and until the value is given the lane lets every call through — a subagent whose files differ on each run is held to the files of its own run.
- A subagent told by a lane at its end and answering in words alone, with no call since, is let end — it is told, never held for good, and its reply reaches the one who started it.
- The lanes you approve are written into your plugin word for word, the sentence at the end of its step and one hook for each subagent and event in `hooks/hooks.json`, your other hooks left as they were, with lane's code in `hooks/lane/`; laid again, the sentence of each lane no longer laid on a step is taken out of it, and the hooks of a subagent your plugin no longer has are taken out of `hooks/hooks.json` — what your subagent is told on a drift is the sentence its step says, and no hook is left for you to remove by hand.
- Run where an older copy of lane's code is, `lane:up` first brings the copy and its hooks up to its own version with the same lanes, asking you nothing, and says so in its first reply; a lane this version no longer lays is put to you, to lay again or leave out — your lanes keep working across lane's updates, and none you approved is dropped unsaid.
- A lane whose step no longer says its sentence stops the subagent's calls and says to run `lane:up` again — a step you changed is not held to what it no longer says.
- The reply_in lane, laid at the end of the step of a skill whose script first prints the language, such as rn's `conversation-language`, keeps the conversation the skill runs in going when it ends a reply in another language, judged by the scripts its prose is written in, and tells it `<plugin>: Reply in <language>.` with the language on the next line; it is written with one `Stop` hook for the skill in `hooks/hooks.json`, lets every reply end until the script has printed the language, lets the next reply end once told, and without Python lets every reply end unchecked — the person is answered in the language they chose, not in the language of what the AI last read.
- Without Python 3.9 or later run as `python3`, each hook on a tool call stops the calls it watches and asks for Python to be installed, and each hook at a subagent's end tells you so and lets the subagent end — a lane is never left unkept with no one told, and a subagent is not kept going on what it cannot put right.
